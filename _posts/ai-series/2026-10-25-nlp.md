# Natural Language Processing (NLP)

<div markdown="1" style="border-left:4px solid #1a5c9e;background:#f0f4f8;padding:14px 18px;margin:18px 0;border-radius:6px;">

Natural Language Processing

**Natural Language Processing (NLP)** is a subfield of AI concerned with enabling computers to understand, interpret, and generate human language. It bridges the gap between human communication and computer understanding.

## Key Tasks in NLP

- **Tokenization**: Splitting text into words or subword units.

- **Parsing**: Analyzing grammatical structure (syntax trees).

- **Named Entity Recognition (NER)**: Identifying entities like persons, organizations, locations.

- **Sentiment Analysis**: Determining the emotional tone of text.

- **Machine Translation**: Translating between languages (e.g., Google Translate).

- **Question Answering**: Answering questions posed in natural language.

- **Text Summarization**: Generating concise summaries of longer texts.

- **Speech Recognition**: Converting spoken language to text.

- **Relation Extraction**: Identifying relationships between entities in text.

## Applications of NLP

- Chatbots and virtual assistants (Siri, Alexa, ChatGPT)

- Search engines and information retrieval

- Machine translation systems (Google Translate, DeepL)

- Spam detection and text classification

- Medical record analysis and clinical decision support

- Legal document review and contract analysis

NLP in Action

Consider the sentence: *“Apple Inc. purchased a startup in Cupertino.”*

NLP tasks applied:

- **Tokenization**: \[Apple, Inc., purchased, a, startup, in, Cupertino, .\]

- **NER**: Apple Inc. (ORG), Cupertino (LOC)

- **POS Tagging**: Apple (NNP), purchased (VBD), a (DT), startup (NN), in (IN), Cupertino (NNP)

- **Relation Extraction**: (, )

- **Sentiment**: Neutral (factual statement)

## FOL and NLP Connection

First-order logic provides a formal representation for the meaning of natural language sentences. FOL can represent:

- Quantified statements (“All students study AI” $\rightarrow$ $\forall x \; \text{Student}(x) \Rightarrow \text{Studies}(x, \text{AI})$)

- Relationships (“John knows Mary” $\rightarrow$ $\text{Knows}(\text{John}, \text{Mary})$)

- Complex logical structure (“If it rains, the ground gets wet” $\rightarrow$ $\text{Rains} \Rightarrow \text{WetGround}$)

NLP systems often use logical forms as intermediate representations for semantic understanding. FOL-based inference enables question answering and reasoning over the extracted meaning.
